Knee replacement is usually the last step, not the first — recommended only once conservative treatment (weight management, physiotherapy, medication, injections) has been tried for months without lasting relief. If you've genuinely tried three or more of these for six-plus months and you're still struggling, these seven signs tell you it's time for a specialist opinion.

## First, the Conservative Ladder

Before surgery is even discussed, most patients with [knee osteoarthritis](/conditions/osteoarthritis) will have tried:

- Weight management
- Quadriceps-strengthening exercises
- NSAIDs (paracetamol, ibuprofen) under medical supervision
- Physiotherapy
- Intra-articular injections — steroid or hyaluronic acid
- Activity modification — cushioned footwear, avoiding deep squatting and stairs where possible

If you've tried three or more of these for six months or longer without lasting relief, keep reading.

## 7 Signs It's Time to Consult a Knee Specialist

1. **Pain wakes you up at night.** Bone-on-bone friction inside an arthritic joint doesn't quiet down with rest the way muscular pain does — it's a sign the joint itself, not just the surrounding tissue, has worn down.
2. **You can't walk 500 metres without stopping.** This is a measurable, honest way to track functional decline — note the distance and revisit it every few months to see the trend.
3. **Pain medication isn't lasting.** If the NSAIDs that used to get you through the day no longer touch the pain, or you need them daily just to function, the underlying damage has likely progressed past what medication can manage.
4. **You've stopped doing things you love.** Gardening, temple visits, family outings, sitting on the floor for a puja — when knee pain starts quietly editing your life, that's a real clinical signal, not just an inconvenience.
5. **The knee gives way without warning.** Instability — the knee suddenly buckling — is more than uncomfortable; it's dangerous, and it usually points to ligament or cartilage damage that needs proper evaluation.
6. **Visible deformity is getting worse.** Bow-legs (varus) or knock-knees (valgus) that are progressing, not stable, usually reflect one-sided cartilage loss.
7. **X-rays already show severe arthritis.** Specifically, Grade 3 or 4 on the Kellgren-Lawrence scale — the standard grading system radiologists use, also referenced on our [osteoarthritis page](/conditions/osteoarthritis).

## What "Grade 3/4 Arthritis" Actually Looks Like on X-ray

The Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) scale grades osteoarthritis severity from an X-ray:

- **Grade 0** — normal joint
- **Grade 1** — mild changes, joint space still looks normal
- **Grade 2** — small bone spurs (osteophytes) appear
- **Grade 3** — clear joint space narrowing with multiple spurs
- **Grade 4** — bone-on-bone contact, cartilage essentially gone

Surgery is typically *discussed* at Grade 3 and *recommended* at Grade 4 — but grading is only one input. Your pain, your function, and what conservative treatment has already failed matter just as much as the X-ray itself.

## Two Age Myths Worth Retiring

**"I'm too young for knee replacement."** Modern implants are designed to last two decades or more. Replacing a knee at 55 means a possible revision around 75 — a well-established, doable procedure. Living in daily pain for another 15–20 years to "save" the knee for later isn't automatically the better trade.

**"I'm too old for surgery."** Patients well into their 80s have safe outcomes when their overall health — particularly cardiac fitness — supports it. Age alone is rarely the deciding factor; general health is.

## What Happens at a Knee Specialist Consultation

- A detailed pain history — when it started, where it hurts, what makes it worse
- A physical exam — range of motion, stability, alignment
- X-ray review, often including a fresh weight-bearing (standing) X-ray, since the knee joint space narrows differently under load
- An honest discussion of options — conservative treatment may still be worth trying further
- If surgery is appropriate: which implant, which technique — see our comparison of [robotic vs conventional knee replacement](/robotic-vs-conventional-knee-replacement) — and a realistic recovery plan

## How to Prepare for Your Consultation

- Bring existing X-rays and any reports you have
- List every medication and treatment you've tried, and for how long
- Write down your top three daily limitations — "can't sit for puja," "can't climb the stairs to the temple," "can't walk to the market" — specifics help a surgeon understand your real-world impact
- Bring a family member — this decision affects them too, and a second set of ears helps
