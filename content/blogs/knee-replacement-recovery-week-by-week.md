Recovery from knee replacement is more predictable than most patients fear. With Dr. Dheeraj Dubay's Zero technique, most patients stand and take their first steps within 24 hours of surgery. Here's a realistic, no-sugarcoating timeline of what to actually expect from Day 0 through Month 6 — and the red flags that mean you should call the clinic.

## Day 0 — Surgery Day

- Anaesthesia is usually a spinal block with sedation
- Surgery itself takes roughly 60–90 minutes for a single knee
- You'll spend a few hours in the recovery room before being moved to your ward bed
- Pain is present but managed with IV medication as part of the [Zero technique](/procedures/zero-technique-knee-replacement) multimodal pain protocol
- Most patients are settled in their room by evening

## Day 1

- The physiotherapist comes to your bedside — this is the milestone day
- First standing, and a few steps with a walking frame
- IV painkillers continue alongside the nerve-block effect from surgery
- Dressing check; any drain is usually removed
- Most patients are genuinely surprised they can stand this soon

## Days 2–3

- Walking 20–30 metres on a flat surface with the walker
- Managing the toilet independently, using a grab bar
- Pain control transitions from IV to oral medication
- Supervised practice on 2–3 stairs

## Discharge — Typically Day 3–4

- Walking 50+ metres independently with the walker
- Knee bend progressing well past the immediate post-op stiffness
- Going home with pain medication and a follow-up date already booked
- Home setup checklist: a raised toilet seat or bedside commode, a bed you can get in and out of easily, no loose rugs or trailing wires underfoot

## Week 1 at Home

- Prescribed exercises 4–5 times a day — your physiotherapist gives you a written sheet, follow it exactly
- Ice and elevation, 3–4 times daily
- Some patients move from a walker to a cane by the end of this week — others take a little longer, and that's normal
- Avoid: sitting cross-legged, squatting, lifting anything heavy

## Week 2

- Stitches or staples are typically removed around day 10–14
- Most patients are now walking around the house without much support
- Knee bend continues to improve
- Driving: not yet — wait until around week 6, and only once your surgeon confirms your reflexes and strength are ready

## Week 4

- Light household chores become manageable
- A cane becomes optional for many patients indoors
- Stairs start feeling routine rather than effortful
- Follow-up visit at the OPD

## Week 6

- Most desk-based jobs are workable again
- Driving an automatic car is often fine from here — confirm with Dr. Dubay first
- Walking a kilometre or more in a day becomes realistic
- Knee bend is well past the stiff early stage by now

## Weeks 8–12

- Most patients report being at 85–90% of full function
- Pre-surgery routines start resuming — puja, temple visits, light gardening
- Longer travel becomes comfortable
- Visible swelling has mostly resolved

## Month 4–6: Full Recovery

- The implant is fully integrated with the surrounding bone
- Most patients can manage a low chair or supported squat
- Swimming, brisk walking, and cycling are back on the table
- Long-term: avoid running on hard surfaces and contact sports — the implant is built for daily life, not high-impact sport

## Red Flags — When to Call the Clinic Immediately

- Sudden, severe swelling in the calf or leg — could signal a blood clot (DVT)
- Fever above 100°F
- Wound that's oozing, red, or has an odour
- Pain that suddenly worsens after it had been improving
- Difficulty breathing — this is an emergency; go to the ER, don't wait for a callback

## How to Recover Faster

- Do the physiotherapy exercises exactly as prescribed — consistency beats intensity
- Keep your weight in a healthy range — every extra kilogram is extra load on the new joint
- Don't push too hard too early; overdoing week 2 can set back week 4
- Sleep with a pillow under your calf, not directly under the knee, to avoid it stiffening in a bent position
- Stay hydrated and eat protein-rich food to support tissue healing
